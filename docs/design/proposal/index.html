Design proposal · 8. September 2026

Mini-Monopoly Schwiiz

Zürich · Basel · Frick · Sursee. Browser board game, solo against the CPU or 2–4 players peer-to-peer, guaranteed to finish inside 30 minutes.

Approved 8 September 2026: all seven decisions taken with the recommended option. Name: Hüüsli-Jagd. Editions are separate boards, never mixed. Build in progress.

Decisions (settled)

Kept for the record. Each was answered with the first option.

  1. Stack: follow the game-* house pattern instead of Vite + TypeScript + PeerJS?
    Your stack overlay for game repos forbids bundlers, package.json and PeerJS. Tschau Sepp ships buildless vanilla JS on GitHub Pages. This proposal follows that. Override on purpose if you prefer the earlier choice.
  2. Signalling: copy-paste offer/answer codes like Tschau Sepp?
    With four players the host exchanges three code pairs. Proven and serverless, but the most friction in the whole game. Room codes would need PeerJS, which the house rule bans.
  3. Name and repo slug.
    “Monopoly” is a trademark, so the public name should avoid it. Candidates: Hüüsli-Jagd, Chaufe & Baue, Stadt · Land · Miete. Repo: game-<slug>.
  4. What does “Rega” in the folder name refer to?
    If there is a physical Rega-branded board game you want to clone, photos of its board and cards let me match the real squares and deck instead of the invented ones below.
  5. Rules: majority-build (2 of 3 streets) and no trading or auctions in v1?
    This is what makes sets happen without negotiation. Trading over P2P is the single most complex UI and is parked.
  6. Defaults: round limit 20 plus a hard 30-minute clock, both completing the current round?
    Winner by net worth when either limit hits. The simulation harness will tune the number.
  7. Language: Schwiizerdütsch only, or gsw + en from day one?
    Tschau Sepp is Schwiizerdütsch only. Your base rule asks for de + en for text-heavy apps.

Why it ends in 30 minutes

Classic Monopoly runs long because full colour sets are rare and cash never runs dry. Four levers fix that.

24 squares, not 40

Six per side. A lap takes three to four turns, so property sells out in the first few rounds.

Majority rule

Build once you own 2 of a city's 3 streets. All 3 doubles the base rent on top. No trading required.

Hard end

After 20 rounds or 30 minutes, whichever first, finishing the round. Highest net worth wins.

No dead time

Rent, tax, salary and cards resolve themselves. A human only answers: buy? build? how do you pay?

Budget: 4 players × 20 rounds = 80 turns. CPU turns take about 2 seconds, human turns 15 to 20. Two humans and two CPUs land around 25 minutes; the clock caps the rest.

Look: a travel case on the table

Reference: Hasbro's Monopoly Kompakt travel edition (Orell Füssli). A red plastic case, a pale green board photographed tilted in three-quarter view, chunky green houses and red hotels standing on the squares, white dice, metal tokens, title deeds fanned beside it. The game is drawn as that object, not as a diagram.

Pieces are things

A bought house pops onto its square as a block. Dice tumble as cubes. Tokens slide along the perimeter square by square.

Cards stay flat

Tap or hover a square and its Besitzrechtkarte lifts as a 2D card facing you. The board carries atmosphere, the card carries the numbers.

Swiss tokens

Postauto, Bernhardiner, Rega-Heli, Pedalo replace car, dog, ship and cat. Simple SVG silhouettes, one colour each.

Flat view toggle

One switch removes the tilt for phones in portrait, screen readers and anyone who finds it hard to read. Motion respects reduced-motion settings.

Four editions, one board

Zürich, Basel, Frick and Sursee are separate editions, never mixed on one board. The player picks the edition in the menu. All four share one template: the same square types, prices and rent tiers at the same positions, only the names change. So the engine, the CPU and the interface never branch on the edition. Rules

  • Start. 1500 CHF each, everyone on LOS, random start player. Salary 200 for passing LOS.
  • Turn. Roll two dice, move, resolve the square, then optional actions (build, mortgage, unmortgage), end turn. Doubles roll again; a third double sends you to jail.
  • Unowned property. Buy at list price or pass. No auction; a passed property stays with the bank.
  • Rent. Paid automatically. Streets by house level, full city doubles the level-0 rent, stations by count owned, utility 8 × roll. Mortgaged property collects nothing.
  • Building. Allowed on a street when you own at least 2 of that city's 3. Levels 0 to 4 (3 Hüüser + Hotel). Build as many as cash allows per turn. No even-build rule, no house supply limit.
  • Mortgage. Raises half the price; unmortgage at 55 %. Houses are sold first at half house cost.
  • Jail. Pay 50 at the start of the turn or try for doubles; after two failed tries you pay and move. Visiting is free.
  • Debt. A payment that drives cash below zero opens Gäldnot: sell houses or mortgage until solvent, or declare bankruptcy. Bankrupt to a player: the creditor gets everything, properties arrive mortgaged. Bankrupt to the bank: properties return unowned.
  • End. Last solvent player, or after round 20 / the 30-minute clock with the current round completed. Net worth = cash + property prices + house costs, mortgaged property counts half. Ties go to cash.

CPU opponents

  • Gmüetlich. Buys when it can afford price + 100, seven times out of ten. Builds at random. Always pays out of jail.
  • Gwieft. Keeps a reserve of 150 plus the highest rent it could hit next lap. Buys anything that gives it 2 of 3 in a city or denies an opponent's 2 of 3. Values stations by count owned. Builds the city with the best rent per CHF up to level 3 while the reserve holds. Sits in jail late in the game when the board is dangerous.

Both are pure functions of state and seed, so the simulation harness can replay every decision.

Architecture

Buildless ES modules, matching the game-* stack overlay. No package.json, no bundler.

src/i18n.js             strings { gsw, en }, CHF via Intl.NumberFormat('de-CH')

One pure reducer. Dice come from the random generator inside the state, so a game is fully determined by its seed and action list. That gives replay for free, an engine testable in Node without extracting scripts from HTML, and a trivially host-authoritative network model.

Multiplayer: host-authoritative star, as in Tschau Sepp

  • Host makes one offer code per guest, guest returns an answer code. Copy-paste signalling, STUN only, no server. The deflate + base64 code format and lobby handling from Tschau Sepp can be lifted nearly verbatim.
  • Seats have a kind: local, cpu, remote. The host runs the reducer and CPU moves; guests only render.
  • Guest sends {t:'act', action}. Host validates it against the legal actions, applies it, broadcasts the full state with the recipient's seat.
  • A dropped guest becomes left: its turn is skipped and its property is frozen. Fewer than two seats ends the game.
  • Because seat kinds are free, hotseat play on one screen costs nothing and closes Tschau Sepp's open TODO for local multiplayer.

Interface

  • 7 × 7 CSS grid: 24 perimeter cells, centre holds the dice, the last event and the action buttons. Wrapped in a perspective container with rotateX(58deg) rotateZ(-38deg) for the isometric scene; the case sides are pseudo-elements folded down with rotateX(90deg) and rotateY(90deg). Player panel with cash and coloured property chips beside it, below it on phones.
  • Houses, hotels and dice are three-face CSS cubes placed with translateZ; tokens are SVG standees counter-rotated to face the camera. No canvas, no engine, the DOM stays the source of truth.
  • Round counter and 30-minute clock always visible; the final round is announced.
  • Schwiizerdütsch copy, en strings through the house i18n pattern, amounts through Intl.NumberFormat.
  • Web Audio sounds for dice, cash and jail, muteable. Polish, not core.

Testing

The stack is buildless, so the gate is the manual playtest checklist plus a Node harness that imports the engine directly. Invariants after every action: cash and bank ledger balance, no house without the majority, no negative cash outside Gäldnot, every game ends within the round limit. Over 1000 CPU-vs-CPU games it reports rounds to finish, bankruptcies and winner spread by seat order. Those numbers tune prices, salary and the round limit until the 30-minute target holds.
